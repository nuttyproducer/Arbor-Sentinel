## Legal Terminology Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|
| "The Commission found that acts constitute genocide" used throughout — never "Genocide has been committed" | COI findings are attributed to the Commission. The platform reports findings — it does not independently find or assert legal conclusions. |
| "Starvation as a method of warfare is the subject of an active ICC arrest warrant" not "Starvation has been used as a method of warfare" | The ICC arrest warrant establishes reasonable grounds — it is a judicial finding at the warrant stage, not a final conviction. |
| "Deliberate targeting" attributed to COI, not asserted by platform | The platform reports the COI's finding. It does not verify or endorse it. |
| IHL obligations described using "must" / "is prohibited" — these are treaty norms, not case findings | States' treaty obligations under IHL are statements of law, not disputed factual claims. |
| Protection categories describe IHL special protections, then document what primary sources report about breaches | Structural separation: IHL norms (statements of law) → source-reported facts about breaches (attributed findings). |

## Open Questions

1. **CPJ Source Entry:** The Journalists & Media Workers protection category has no sourceIds. A CPJ-specific data source should be added for journalist casualty documentation. Should this be done before sign-off?
2. **Casualty Data Methodology:** The UN COI and OCHA use different casualty data methodologies. Should the dossier include a note explaining the differences?
3. **Ceasefire Status:** The dossier currently uses past tense for the October 2025 ceasefire ("entered into effect") and describes its collapse ("ceasefire collapses; Israel blocks aid"). Is this accurate enough? The ceasefire's exact status is complex and evolving.
4. **Food Insecurity vs. Famine:** The IPC July 2026 snapshot classifies Gaza at Phase 3 (Crisis), not Phase 5 (Famine). The March 2024 assessment found famine was "projected and imminent." Should the dossier explain the IPC phase distinctions?
5. **Protection Category Scope:** Should additional categories be added for: (a) detainees, (b) persons with disabilities, (c) older persons?
6. **Action Jurisdiction Scoping:** The lawful action pathways are currently scoped generically. Should they include specific Belgian parliamentary committee references, MEP contact templates, etc.?
7. **Evidence Linking:** Should each harm category link directly to specific evidence records when they become available?

### Non-trivial judgment calls made during preparation:

1. **Attribution of genocide finding:** The UN COI's June 2026 report finding is always attributed: "The Commission found that acts constitute genocide." The platform does not state "Genocide has been committed" or "Israel has committed genocide" as a platform assertion. Legal reviewer must confirm this attribution pattern is sufficient.

2. **IPC food-security language:** Using "IPC Phase 3 (Crisis)" rather than "famine" or "starvation." The IPC phase terminology is technical and defined. Reviewer should confirm the distinction between "food insecurity" and "famine" is clear to non-expert readers.

3. **"Obliterated" language:** The ICRC's own word ("obliterated") is used in direct quotes. Reviewer should confirm quoting the ICRC's characterisation is appropriate rather than paraphrasing.
