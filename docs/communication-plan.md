# Communication Plan — Public Launch

**Status:** Pre-launch (M7-06)  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-04  
**Owned by:** Spokesperson + editorial lead

This plan governs how Accountability Atlas communicates about the public
launch. It complements `docs/communications-playbook.md` (tone, pitches,
crisis posture) and `docs/outreach.md` (partner/contributor outreach).

---

## 1. Spokesperson designation

The project has **one authorized spokesperson** for all public communication
about the project and any incidents. All external statements — press
responses, social media posts on official accounts, and incident updates —
are issued or explicitly approved by this person before publication.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Role | **Spokesperson** (sole authorized external voice) |
| Name | [Named individual — must be filled in and authorized in writing before launch] |
| Title | [e.g., Project coordinator / Founder] |
| Contact | [email] |
| Deputies | [Named individual] — may speak only with explicit spokesperson approval |
| Written authorization | [Link to signed authorization / decision-log entry] |

**Rules for the spokesperson:**
- All press enquiries route to the spokesperson (press contact below).
- No contributor speaks to the press or posts official announcements without
  spokesperson approval.
- During an incident, the spokesperson coordinates with the incident lead and
  legal counsel (see `docs/incident-response-plan.md`).
- The spokesperson does not speculate; they confirm facts and point to
  published material.

**Press contact:** [press@] mailbox, monitored by the spokesperson.
Monitor and archive all press enquiries; record permission/attribution
status privately (see `docs/outreach.md`).

## 3. Talking points

Approved talking points. Stay on these; do not ad-lib claims about scale,
partnerships, or status.

### Core message
> Accountability Atlas is an open-source civic-technology platform that
> organizes verified public evidence, tracks legal and political
> responsibility, and helps people take lawful action for civilian
> protection — starting with Gaza and the wider regional crisis.

### What we emphasize
- Evidence and source methodology — every record carries a source and a review status.
- Lawful action — we never solicit unlawful or harmful action.
- Civilian protection and humanitarian access — the purpose of the platform.
- Open source and corrections — the public can review and correct the content.
- Do-no-harm principles — safe coordinates, no doxxing, no hate content.
- Modest scope — a public beta, not a court, authority, or final arbiter.

### What we avoid
- Revenge, harassment, or collective-blame framing.
- Claims of being a court, an official authority, or a legal adviser.
- Implying partnership or endorsement without written confirmation.
- Overstating content volume or review completeness beyond what is published.
- "We speak for victims" or any claim to represent affected communities.

### Key facts to include (accurate as of launch)
- Open-source civic-tech project, static public beta.
- Documented, reviewed content across evidence, legal tracker, countries,
  institutions, organizations, action templates, and dossiers.
- Corrections and bug reporting routes are open.
- No user accounts, no tracking scripts, no witness submissions, no evidence
  uploads during the static beta (see `docs/privacy-principles.md`).

## 4. FAQ

### What is Accountability Atlas?
An open-source civic-technology platform for organizing verified public
evidence, tracking legal and political responsibility, and enabling lawful
public action — starting with Gaza and the wider regional crisis.

### Is this a campaign against a people?
No. The project opposes harm to civilians, atrocity crimes, humanitarian
obstruction, and impunity. It does not target identity groups or civilians.

### Is this a legal authority or a court?
No. The platform organizes public records, legal developments, NGO
conclusions, and evidence sources. Courts and competent legal bodies make
legal determinations. We are not a law firm and do not give legal advice.

### Are the listed organizations partners?
Not unless clearly confirmed in writing. Most organization directory entries
are public resource listings only, with a visible disclaimer.

### Does the platform accept witness evidence?
Not yet. Sensitive submissions require expert-reviewed security, legal,
privacy, and human-rights workflows.

### Who is behind it?
Accountability Atlas is built by a group of contributors. The named
spokesperson is the contact for press. The project is open source on GitHub.

### How can I correct an error?
Use the `/corrections` route or open a GitHub Issue. Corrections are part of
the trust model; see `docs/correction-policy.md`.

### How can I contribute?
See the `/contribute` route and the GitHub repository.

### How is my privacy handled?
The static beta has no user accounts, no tracking scripts, and no analytics.
See `docs/privacy-principles.md`.

## 5. Prepared statements

These are pre-approved position statements. Use them verbatim or lightly
adapted. Do not improvise beyond them without spokesperson + legal review.

### 5.1 General backlash / criticism of the project

> Accountability Atlas is an open-source project with a published
> methodology, transparent sourcing, and a public corrections route. We
> welcome scrutiny: if a source, translation, or legal characterization is
> wrong, we want to know and will correct it in line with our correction
> policy. We are not a court or an authority — we organize verified public
> evidence and lawful action pathways. We take allegations of error or
> harm seriously and review every correction submitted.

### 5.2 Allegation that we target a group or incite harm

> We do not target identity groups or civilians, and we do not solicit
> unlawful or harmful action. Our methodology is published and our
> anti-hate and anti-doxxing rules are explicit. We review all content for
> safety and legal compliance before publication. If you believe any page
> crosses these lines, please use the corrections route or contact us
> directly.

### 5.3 Allegation of false partnership / endorsement

> We do not claim partnership or endorsement from any organization unless
> that organization has confirmed the relationship in writing. Organizations
> listed in our directory are included as public resources so users can find
> official information. We are happy to correct any entry that an
> organization believes misrepresents them.

### 5.4 Content error discovered publicly

> Thank you for flagging this. We are reviewing the source and wording. If a
> correction is needed, we will update the page and note the change according
> to our correction policy.

### 5.5 "Why is the content incomplete / only in English?" (scope defense)

> Accountability Atlas is a public beta. We publish what is reviewed and
> verified, and we label review status honestly on every record. Coverage
> grows as content is reviewed. We do not publish unreviewed material as if
> it were final.

## 6. Communication rules

1. All statements come from the spokesperson (or with explicit approval).
2. Confirm facts before posting; link to published pages rather than claiming.
3. Never disclose private contributor or organization communications.
4. During an incident, external statements follow `docs/incident-response-plan.md` and are issued only after containment and legal review.
5. Correct errors promptly and publicly; the correction is part of the record.
6. No engagement in quote-tweet fights or pile-ons (see `docs/communications-playbook.md`).
7. Coordinate with the incident lead and legal counsel before any statement about a security or data incident.
